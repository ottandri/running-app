Zones are from your clinical lactate test (Leistungsdiagnostik SOMC Schulthess Klinik, 02.06.2025): an 8-stage treadmill lactate profile with your lab-measured threshold of 178 bpm at 14.5 km/h, and max HR of 197 bpm reached at exhaustion. This matches Garmin's independently auto-detected lactate threshold of 178 bpm exactly, and the lab's VO2max estimate (58 ml/kg/min) is nearly identical to Garmin's (57–58) — strong agreement between both sources.

Polarized 80/20: roughly 80% of weekly running time stays in REG/GA1/GA2 (genuinely easy to steady), and about 20% is spent at EB/INT (genuinely hard) — deliberately minimizing time in the mushy "medium-hard" zone most recreational runners default to. It's the pattern most elite endurance athletes, Kipchoge included, actually train by.

Structure at a glance. Deload weeks land every 4 weeks (4 and 8) — your lab report actually suggests an even more conservative 2:1 cycle given your workload, worth considering if week 4 doesn't feel like enough of a reset. Half-marathon-pace work enters the long run from week 4 (seven weeks out) and builds to the peak session in week 10. Taper is 8 days exactly: from Sunday of week 10 through race day.

Fueling. Any long run tagged "Fuel from 15 km" is a rehearsal for race-day nutrition — aim for 30–60 g carbs/hour from that point, whatever gel or drink you plan to use on the day. Don't try anything new in the final 1–2 weeks.
