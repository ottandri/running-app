Training plan · 9 Nov 2026 – 11 Apr 2027

Zurich Marathon

22 weeks, 4 runs a week, 2 strength sessions and 3 short mobility sessions from day one. A 4-week return-to-run base, then an 18-week marathon block — 2 easy runs, one interval/tempo session alternating week to week, and a long run that grows from 10→30 km. Two B-races (Zürcher Silvesterlauf, Engadin Skimarathon) and a 5K time trial built in.

Same lab-pinned zones as the Lausanne plan (Leistungsdiagnostik SOMC Schulthess Klinik, 02.06.2025): lactate threshold 178 bpm at 14.5 km/h, max HR 197 bpm, matching Garmin's own independently-detected threshold. These HR bands don't move without new lab data.

Pace guide: carried over from the Lausanne plan's most recent recalibration (GA1 retuned to 5:15–6:15/km on 23 Aug 2026 from two weeks of synced data). Free to drift further as this block's own data comes in — recalibrate whenever synced sessions consistently show a different pace at the same HR, same rule as before.

Training philosophy: genuinely easy easy days, genuinely hard hard days — roughly 80% of weekly running time in REG/GA1/GA2, about 20% at EB/marathon pace. Four running days a week, max — your own injury history (pain when running >4×/week) is a hard constraint here, not a preference.

Mobility routines A & B · 20 min each

Three short sessions a week — Mon · Wed · Fri (race week: Tue + Thu) — in the evening or right after training, never in the hour before a quality or long run. Stretch to mild tension only, never into pain. They tick off just for you: they don't count toward the sessions bar and earn nothing on the Runny Farm.

Start at goal pace, not faster. Fuel from the start: a gel every ≈40 min, 60–90 g carbs/hour — exactly what you rehearsed in weeks 11–21.

Your default week: Mon GA1 easy · Tue strength · Wed quality (alternates interval / tempo week to week) · Thu strength · Fri GA1 easy · Sat rest · Sun long run. Mobility (Mon/Wed/Fri, Tue+Thu in race week) goes in the evening or right after training, never in the hour before a hard session. Swap freely when life intervenes — just keep Saturday clear and the long run on Sunday.

Structure at a glance. Four weeks of easy return-to-run first, then an 18-week marathon block: base (wk 5–10), build with marathon-pace work entering the long run (wk 11–17, peaking at 30 km with a 10 km @ pace finish), the Engadin bridge (wk 18–19, a 5–6 h fun ski day instead of a long run, then a recovery week), and a final push (wk 20–21) before an 8-day taper into race day.

B-races. Zürcher Silvesterlauf (13 Dec, week 5) is a short season-opener in place of that week's long run. Engadin Skimarathon (14 Mar, week 18) is deliberately low-HR and just for fun — it replaces that week's long run entirely, not a goal effort. The 5K time trial (week 9, 4 Jan) is the one genuinely maximal effort in the block — goal sub-20:00.

Fueling. Any long run tagged with a marathon-pace block is also a rehearsal for race-day nutrition — 60–90 g carbs/hour from early on, whatever gel or drink you plan to use on the day. Don't try anything new in the final 1–2 weeks.
